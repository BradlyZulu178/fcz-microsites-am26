African bush elephant

Behaviors

African bush elephants are very social animals and usually live in groups called herds. The herds are often led by an older female called a matriarch. They show affection by touching each other with their trunks, cuddling, and staying close together. They use their trunks to greet one another and comfort younger elephants. Elephants also play by chasing each other, rolling in the mud, and spraying water. When they feel threatened, they may spread their ears, raise their trunks, or stomp their feet to warn others. They also protect their babies by keeping them close to the herd.

The behaviors to look out for in an African elephants using their long trunks to pick up food, such as grass, leaves, and fruit, or to drink water. They may also use their trunks to spray water over their bodies to cool down. Another behavior to look for is elephants shaking their heads, flapping their ears, or stomping their feet when they feel upset, annoyed, or threatened. These actions can be ways of showing their emotions or warning other animals to stay away. You may also notice elephants touching each other with their trunks, walking closely together, or protecting their babies as they move around in a herd.

Reproduction and Breeding

African elephants reproduce sexually and have a gestation period of approximately 22 months. Females usually reach sexual maturity around 10 to 12 years old, while males mature later. They typically give birth to one calf at a time, and newborn calves usually weigh around 90 to 120 kg. Female elephants usually have several years between births.

Cool Facts

  • African elephants are the largest land animals
  • Their trunks have around 40,000 muscles and are strong enough to lift heavy objects but gentle enough to pick up small items.
  • Elephants can recognize themselves in mirrors, showing signs of self-awareness.
