Addax

Behaviors

Addax tend to live in small herds or groups. They are more active during the cooler parts of the day, mostly at night or in the early morning. During the hottest parts of the day, they rest in the shade, such as in crevices between boulders, rocks, or bushes. They can survive for long periods without drinking water. They use their horns to defend themselves against predators such as lions, leopards, and cheetahs. Addax also communicate through body movements and sounds.

The behaviors to look out for in the Addax is its tendency to travel slowly in small herds and its habit of digging shallow beds in the sand to escape the daytime heat. Because these desert antelopes are highly adapted to the Sahara, you will also notice they are mostly active at night and rarely drink water, instead getting all their moisture by tracking down sparse vegetation.

Reproduction and Breeding

Addax reproduce sexually, with a gestation period of approximately 8 to 9 months (around 260 days). They usually reach sexual maturity between 1.5 and 2 years of age. Female addax typically give birth to a single calf at a time, with each calf weighing approximately 5 to 7 kg at birth. Females can give birth about once a year, depending on environmental conditions.

Cool Facts

  • The addax never need to drink water, extracting all the moisture they need directly from the plants, grasses, and succulents they eat.
  • Their coats change depending on the season, pure white in the summer to reflect intense sunlight, then shifts to a dark grayish-brown in the winter.
  • The addax are the slowest in the region, making them easy targets for fast predators and vehicles.
